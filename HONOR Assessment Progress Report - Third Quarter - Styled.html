All assessment activities are conducted by the facilitator with authorization from the assessor. The assessment plan is provided to the facilitator by the assessor, who also conducts regular visits to the assessment centre to oversee the process. While some assessment tasks are carried out by the facilitator under the assessor’s supervision, both formative and summative assessments are administered in the presence of the facilitator and, occasionally, the assessor.

Preparation and transparency

Learners undergo thorough preparation for assessment upon completion of each module or set of modules. This promotes transparency in the assessment process. Learners are also required to verify the agenda and topics discussed during the assessment preparation phase.

Integrated delivery

The programme is conducted as a learnership intervention in which 30% of learning is theory and 70% is practical. Learners are being trained in theory and practical concurrently.

Assessment Design and Evaluation

Strategy and plan evaluated against four principles
AreaFairValidReliableWorkable
Strategy The assessment tools used for these particular assessment processes were developed by the assessor in consultation with the moderator. The tools considered the training environment and the level of understanding of enrolled learners. They also ensured that all SO and AC of the unit standard were covered. All assessments were created and conducted according to unit standard details, specific outcomes and assessment criteria. The briefing/training and regular assessment conducted during the course gave learners an understanding of what is required for assessment. The time and assessment venue allocated for each assessment process were sufficient.
Plan Unit standards were divided into separate modules. All learners were given opportunities to understand concepts through facilitation, and barriers to learning were considered. Learners found not competent in certain activities were given a chance to rewrite the specific outcomes. The working plan included workplace assignments and made the work valid through learners’ practical work experience. All assessments were created and aligned with the specific unit standards. The time allocated for each learning area was sufficient.

Design intent: assessment tools, delivery conditions and opportunities for remediation were structured to support fair, valid, reliable and workable assessment.

Assessment Methods and Process

Methods — design evaluation

Fair: Learners did research, discussions, formative assessments, summative assessments and practical workplace assignments.

Valid: Learners were able to complete these assessments, and those who were NYC were given another chance for assessment.

Reliable: Written, observation and oral methods gave the assessor enough information to measure each learner’s competency.

Workable: Assignments were handwritten and others typed. No one had constraints in trying to produce evidence.

Why the assessment process matters

A well-structured assessment process is essential in education and training because it ensures that learning outcomes are measured accurately, fairly and consistently. It provides credible evidence of a learner’s knowledge, skills and competence, confirming whether required standards are met.

Quality

Maintains the quality and credibility of programmes and qualifications.

Improvement

Guides learning by identifying strengths and areas needing support.

Fairness

Ensures transparency through standardised and objective evaluation methods.

Accountability

Supports accountability for training providers, facilitators and learners.

Assessment methodologies: Written questionnaire · Observation · Project Based

Decision support: Informs certification, progression and workplace-readiness decisions.

Assessment Process — Detailed Stages

3.1

Pre-assessment Meeting

A meeting was held between the facilitator and learner to discuss the assessment process. The method was covered during induction, and pre-meetings are held when the assessor visits the training centre. Candidates know what to expect. The assessor, facilitator and learners attend to determine the readiness of all stakeholders.

3.2

Assessment

Assessments conducted thus far used acceptable methods, including written knowledge assessments, research and group work. Evidence submitted by learners was measured against the criteria of each specific outcome for every unit standard assessed.

3.3

Evaluation

Observation and formal marking were used to determine competency or non-competency. These methods were guided by the unit standard details.

3.4

Feedback

Each assessment process had its own feedback session. Candidates are emailed individual feedback, with further discussion arranged between the assessor and candidates where needed.

3.5

Reviewing

The assessor and candidate review the entire process to determine that all stakeholders are treated fairly, without discrimination. Learners provide a written review of each assessment so that they can voice any unfairness.

3.6

Re-assessment

Remedial action was administered whenever a learner showed insufficient competence in specific criteria, outcomes or unit standards. After remediation, assessment recommenced and learners who addressed deficiencies were deemed competent.

3.7

Competency

Following the assessment process, the assessor made a judgement of learners’ competence based on the assessment criteria, specific outcome or unit standard, depending on relevance. This approach supports comprehensive competence across all elements of the learning programme.
